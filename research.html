Medical & Healthcare Robotics

Major research projects

Selected projects developed at KIMM and in earlier academic and industrial research.

CURRENT

Imitation Learning-based Automation of Swab Sampling

Task-level automation of nasopharyngeal swab sampling using imitation learning and visuomotor policies. The work investigates how expert insertion strategies and visually observable procedural knowledge can be reproduced by a robot.

  • ACT-based visuomotor policy learning
  • Learning from teleoperated expert demonstrations
  • Extension toward VLA-based task understanding and action generation
2020–2024

Robot-assisted Remote Swab Sampling System

A master-slave robotic system for remote upper-respiratory specimen collection, designed to reduce direct contact between healthcare workers and patients while supporting accurate and repeatable nasopharyngeal sampling.

  • Remote-center-of-motion mechanism for safe swab insertion
  • Teleoperation and non-face-to-face specimen collection
  • Nonclinical and clinical evaluation for COVID-19 testing

Remote ENT Robot System

Tele-operated robotic technologies for otolaryngology diagnosis, including endoscopic examination and remote manipulation. The research combines compact robotic mechanisms, visual feedback, and safe remote operation.

KIMM

RADIUS — Robot-Assisted Diagnostic Imaging for UltraSound

A tele-robotic ultrasound diagnostic platform for remote sonography. The project explores master-slave manipulation, probe positioning, image-guided operation, and communication over networked environments.

  • Tele-echography and remote ultrasound imaging
  • Probe manipulation and force-aware interaction
  • Image-based guidance and robot-assisted diagnosis
2018–2024

Image-based Robotic Prosthetic Hand Control

Vision-based control of a robotic prosthetic hand using deep-learning-based object detection and integrated 2D/3D visual information to select grasp targets, timing, and pose.

A positioning robot for non-invasive ultrasound neuro-stimulation, focusing on accurate placement, orientation, repeatability, and safe access around the patient.
